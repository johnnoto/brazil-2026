    How the tie lines and estimate work

    Tie line. Start with each state's split between Lula and Flávio on October 4 (other candidates left out). Add the extra movement that state showed between the two rounds in 2022, compared with the country as a whole. Then shift every state by the same amount so the country adds up to exactly 50–50. The result is the share Lula needs in that place to be on track for a tie. Cities and interiors use their own October 4 split with their state's 2022 movement.

    Pace. The live two-candidate share minus the tie line. "Lula +2.0" means Lula is running 2 points better there than a tied race requires; "Flávio +2.0" means the reverse.

    Estimated finish. Each state's result so far is blended with its tie line, adjusted by how far the counted states are running from their lines, and weighted by how many votes the state cast on October 4. It corrects for the fact that states finish counting at different speeds. It is a rough guide, not a projection by the TSE or any news outlet. It can mislead when turnout differs from October 4 or when the early votes inside a state come mostly from its cities.

    Sources. Live and October 4 figures: TSE results feed. 2022 state results: TSE final results as summarized by Santander's post-election report.
